# My Philosophy

Nowadays, there are hundreds of Minecraft modpacks focused on performance, and just as many focused on quality-of-life improvements. Finding one that brings both together into a comfortable, unified experience is a different story.

A purely performance-focused modpack isn't what everyone is looking for. Some players have capable hardware and want more than higher FPS; they want Minecraft to feel more convenient, clean, and enjoyable.

On the other hand, adding too many features can put unnecessary strain on hardware and bring players back to the same problem: finding the right balance.

Truth is, **there isn't a perfect modpack**. It depends on you, your hardware, and what you're looking for.

That's the principle behind Citron. I wanted to combine performance with useful quality-of-life features while staying true to Vanilla Minecraft, rather than focusing entirely on one side.

Simply put, Citron is for players who want a smooth, convenient, clean, and reliable Minecraft experience, and have reasonably capable hardware to make the most of it.

## Integrated Utility

Citron brings together useful mods, with settings and configurations chosen to make them work well together.

The idea is simple: when players download a modpack, they shouldn't have to spend hours searching for everyday features, installing individual mods, and figuring out how to configure everything themselves. **The boring stuff should already be done.**

But this isn't about adding as many mods as possible. Every addition should have a purpose.

## Features over Mods

Mods change. They get updated, abandoned, replaced, or sometimes become unnecessary. That's why I care more about **the feature a mod provides than the mod itself**.

For example, imagine you only want Zoom in Minecraft. You could install a simple zoom mod, or a larger, more popular mod that adds Zoom alongside several camera-overhaul mechanics. If all you need is Zoom, those extra features may be unnecessary.

This is how I approach Citron. I look at what a mod brings to the experience, not just its popularity or how many features it advertises. If another mod provides the same feature more reliably or without unnecessary additions, I'd consider it the better choice.

I believe in the saying **"less is more."** If something adds complexity without providing enough value, it's bloat.
